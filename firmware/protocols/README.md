# protocols

The radio protocols, each a component another project can copy with its tests and build with nothing from skyBlip. [`adsl/`](adsl/README.md) is ADS-L 4 SRD-860 Issue 2, [`adsl_edge/`](adsl_edge/README.md) is what the Go sends on ADS-L's frame that Issue 2 leaves outside its scope, [`adsb/`](adsb/README.md) reads the 1090 MHz extended squitter, [`alptas/`](alptas/README.md) is ALP-TAS, skyBlip's reading of the 2024 air frame, and [`common/`](common/README.md) holds the wire arithmetic two or more of them share, and the figures of EN 300 220, the standard every 868 MHz stack transmits under.

A stack holds what its specification decides. What skyBlip chose stays outside it, in `core/` and `products/`, as adapters that turn a stack's plain values into skyBlip's model and back: `core/protocol/adsl.*` turns own-ship into an ADS-L Traffic payload, `core/protocol/adsb_observation.*` turns an ADS-B track into an `AircraftObservation`, and so on (`core/protocol/README.md`). Where this split was not obvious, the rule decided it: a figure or a formula the specification prints goes in the stack, and a figure skyBlip measured or chose stays out. So ADS-L's stack holds G.1.14's HFOM formula, F.2.2's address tables and §C.5's slots, while the VFOM model, the second of ten a unit owns, the instant a burst is drawn at, where the Status goes, the declared channel-access route and the polite access parameters it declares are skyBlip's, in `core/`. EN 300 220's limits are printed by a standard, so they are in `common/en300220.h`, beside the stacks that cite it: ADS-L §D.3 asks for EN 300 220-1's polite spectrum access by name, and a copy of a stack always takes `common/`. Where the specification sets a rule and leaves a choice inside it, the stack holds the rule and the choice stays out: `adsl::period_s()` is G.1.16's rate, and which seconds and channels own-ship spends it on is `core/timing/`'s.

## The boundary

A file under `protocols/` includes only the C++ standard library, its own directory and `protocols/common/`. It never includes `core/`, `ports/`, `hardware/`, `products/`, `runtime/`, `ui/`, `simulator/` or Zephyr, and one stack never includes another, but for one exception: `adsl_edge/` builds on ADS-L's frame, so it may include `adsl/`, and no stack may include `adsl_edge/`. `core/` and `products/` may include `protocols/`. `scripts/check_protocol_boundary.py` holds every include to this rule, `scripts/test_check_protocol_boundary.py` checks the checker, and CI runs both.

A stack speaks in its own plain types: `adsl::Traffic`, `adsl::StatusReport` and `adsl::UplinkStatistics`, `adsb::Message` and `adsb::Track`, `alptas::Position`, and `wire::Status` for a frame it refuses. Each stack reads a frame off the air through one `receive()`, which runs the CRC and answers `wire::Status`. `adsl::receive()` repairs the bits the demodulator marked, reads the network header and hands back the clear `Packet`. `alptas::receive()` repairs the frame in place, before `decode()` takes its key. `adsb::receive()` repairs nothing, because the LR2021 marks no doubtful chip, and decodes the frame the LR2021 decided into a `Message`. No `model::`, `events::` or `settings::` type appears inside one.

Each stack carries a `README.md` saying what it implements and what it leaves out, a `version.h`, its code and a `test/` folder. Its tests include nothing from skyBlip either. They use the vendored doctest and `common/test/channel.h`, the bit-error channel the frames are pushed through.

## Taking a stack into another project

Copy `protocols/common/` and `protocols/<stack>/` under one include root, keeping the `protocols/` directory, since every include is written from that root as `#include "protocols/adsl/packet.h"`. Compile every `.cpp` in the two directories with C++17, with GCC or Clang: the ADS-L packet is a packed struct with bit fields laid out as GCC lays them, and the stacks call `__builtin_memcpy`, `__builtin_memset` and `__builtin_popcount`. XXTEA works on the frame's bytes copied into 32-bit words, so the host must be little-endian, as the Cortex-M and every host this tree builds on are. The code allocates nothing on the heap and needs neither exceptions nor RTTI. For the tests, add every `.cpp` under their `test/` folders, doctest's header and a main that defines `DOCTEST_CONFIG_IMPLEMENT_WITH_MAIN`.

`make test-protocols` does exactly that for each stack, in a copy under `build/protocols-alone/` that holds the stack, `common/` and `vendor/doctest` and nothing else, and for `adsl_edge/` the ADS-L stack without its tests, so an include that reaches outside fails to compile there. CI runs it. The normal `make test` binary still runs every stack's tests with the rest of the suite, and the device build compiles every stack into the image (`cmake/skyblip.cmake`).

## Versions

Each stack's `version.h` is the one source of its version. `kVersion` is a `major.minor.patch` string and `kSpecification` names what the stack implements. A version moves only when a change alters a rule the protocol's conformance rests on: what the stack decides about its frames, its encoding, its channels, its timing or its transmit rules, or a reading its frozen conformance suite pins. A change inside those rules, such as which second a burst takes, moves the firmware version alone. `firmware/README.md` says how the protocol versions relate to the firmware version. A stack stays at 0.1.0 until it is ready for production, whatever changes in it before then. From then on its version moves by the rule above.

Each product lists the protocols it carries in `products/<product>/PROTOCOLS`, one stack name per line. Every release names the protocol versions each product carries, in a pre-release's notes and in a production release's `PROTOCOLS.md`, read out of `version.h` by `lib/radio_protocols.rb`. A unit reports them too, so a unit in hand can be checked against a declaration of conformity: it answers the companion link's `protocols` command with each stack's `kVersion` (`core/comms/README.md`, The radio protocols), and the release's `PROTOCOLS.md` names the `kSpecification` beside each version. `./scripts/blip.py protocols` prints them, and the device page of the briefing app shows them. The unit reads the version out of `protocols/<stack>/version.h`, for the stacks `products/<product>/radio_protocols.h` lists, and `test/products/test_radio_protocols.cpp` fails when that list and `PROTOCOLS` differ.

`adsl/version.h` is the version of skyBlip's ADS-L implementation, not the Protocol Version field every ADS-L packet carries on air (E.1.1). The ADS-L declaration of conformity names firmware versions, in the template's "Firmware Version" field (`docs/reference/ads-l_4_srd860_issue_2.md:1659`). The ADS-L version decides which firmware releases a declaration still covers, because each release lists the protocol versions it carries.

A version covers its stack's directory and the `protocols/common/` files the stack includes, so a change to either is a decision on that version. A frozen stack makes that decision visible in a `version.lock` beside its `version.h`: the version, the roots its conformance rests on, and the SHA-256 of every file under them and of every `protocols/common/` file they include. `scripts/check_protocol_freeze.py` fails when one of those files changed, appeared or went and the lock was not resealed, `scripts/test_check_protocol_freeze.py` checks the checker, and CI runs both. `python3 scripts/check_protocol_freeze.py --reseal <stack>` rewrites the lock under the current `kVersion`: after moving it, or after a change that leaves what the stack puts on air and reads off it as it was, which the pull request then says. The lock's diff is what review sees.

ADS-L is sealed at 0.1.0 and is declared 1.0.0 in a pull request of its own. Until then a change to a sealed file is resealed without a bump, and the rule above decides from 1.0.0 on. Its lock holds the stack and its half of the conformance suite, the device's half in `test/adsl/`, the EN 300 220-1 suite in `test/en300220/` that §D.3 asks for, the files that set what goes on air without a host case reaching them whole: `core/protocol/air.*` (the M-band receiver, its shared sync window and the length it reads from the sync tail), `hardware/parts/sx1262/sx1262.*` (sync word, packet parameters, conducted power and modulation), `hardware/platform/zephyr/rf.h` and `rf_mailbox.h` (running the assessment `hardware/platform/clear_channel.h` decides, keying the PA at the planned instant, and which bursts may be forced), `pps.h` (the edge every slot is measured from), `clock.h` (the deadlines the executor keys against) and `io.h` (the radio's commands and bytes over SPI), and the two test harnesses the frozen cases fly through, `test/support/simulator_run.h` and `test/support/m_band_rig.h`.

The rest of the firmware stays free. `core/protocol/adsl.*` and `adsl_uplink.*`, `core/timing/`, `core/traffic/table.*`, `core/flight/`, `core/units/`, `core/settings/address.h`, the Go's settings and `RadioService` decide what the Go sends inside the rules: the 20 s cycle, the dwell map, which second and channel each burst takes, the instants, the VFOM model, airborne detection. The device's half of the suite holds them to the rules, and to each reading put to EASA, so a change there that breaks one fails a case under `test/adsl/`, and changing that case means resealing the lock.

`adsl_edge/` is not frozen, and stays at 0.1.0 while what it carries is ahead of the specification (`adsl_edge/README.md`). The Go carries it, and `PROTOCOLS` leaves it out on purpose: that list, the release's `PROTOCOLS.md` and the companion link's `protocols` command exist to hold a unit to its declarations, and no declaration covers payload 66. When an ADS-L issue defines the payload, its record moves into the ADS-L stack under that issue's version, and `adsl_edge/` goes.

ADS-B and ALP-TAS are not frozen yet. These skyBlip files outside each stack change what it puts on air or reads off it, and a change to one of them is a decision on its version:

| Version | skyBlip files outside the stack that change what it puts on air or reads off it |
|---|---|
| ADS-B | `core/protocol/adsb_observation.*`, `core/protocol/adsb_category.*`, `core/units/units.h`, `arctangent` in `core/util/intmath.*` (the track a velocity gives), `adsl::kAddressTableIcao` where `adsb_observation.cpp` writes it, `hardware/parts/lr2021/`, and `products/skyblip_bravo/services/adsb.*` |
| ALP-TAS | `core/protocol/alptas.*`, the ALP-TAS half of `core/protocol/air.*`, `adsl_category_to_alptas_type` in `core/protocol/nmea_out.*`, and `products/skyblip_go/services/traffic.*` (`decode_alptas`) |
