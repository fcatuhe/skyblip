### test/core/test_adsl.cpp

The ADS-L position packet end to end, a radio protocol checked with no RF hardware in the room: the channel is an injectable bit error rate, and the packet has to survive it or say that it did not. What these separate is a *detected failure*, a frame dropped and counted, from a *silent miscorrection*, a frame that decodes to a plausible aircraft somewhere it is not. Only the second one collides with anybody.

**adsl**

- Monte-Carlo BER, detected vs silent miscorrection accounting
- a degrading HDOP walks the accuracy claim down
  > A degrading fix walks the codes down together, and a hopeless one claims nothing rather than claiming a number nobody should act on.
- a flagged bit past the eighth is searched, not wrapped back onto the first
- an address goes out as configured, unless no decoder could read it
  > F4. The address goes on the air here, so this is the last place a stored blob can be caught.
- an over-range altitude encodes the limit, not 'unavailable'
- an over-range ground speed encodes the limit, not 'unavailable'
- an over-range sink rate encodes the limit, not 'unavailable'
- field accessors round-trip through the wire layout
- from_own claims integrity from the receiver's DOP
  > Every field of the Integrity block has a zero code meaning "unknown / no fix" (G.1.11 to G.1.16), so an untouched block is not neutral: it tells a receiver we claim no integrity at all, and receivers are entitled to weight us accordingly. Without a fix that is the truthful answer; with one it is not.
- from_own marks what own-ship does not know
- horizontal accuracy code sits on the G.1.14 boundaries
  > G.1.14 NACp boundaries, in metres of horizontal figure of merit: 7 is under 3 m, 6 under 10 m, 5 under 30 m, 4 under 0.05 NM (92.6 m), and 0 is 0.5 NM or worse, which is the same code as "no fix".
- multi-bit errors within flagged (weak) bits are corrected
- scramble+crc, then descramble+check round-trip is lossless (BER 0)
- single-bit error is corrected via CRC syndrome
- the invalid codes are reachable on purpose and round-trip
- vertical accuracy code sits on the G.1.15 boundaries
  > G.1.15 GVA: 3 under 10 m, 2 under 45 m, 1 under 150 m, 0 beyond.

### test/core/test_air.cpp

**air**

- a foreign sync word frames as nothing
- a report shorter than the dwell reads is refused
- an ADS-L burst frames as ADS-L, byte-aligned on its data
- an ALP-TAS burst frames as ALP-TAS through the same window
- an M-band burst is still framed and named from its sync tail
- an O-band burst carries the sync word, the length byte and the codeword
  > The O-band framing a transmitter uses, round-tripped through the same sync strip the radio performs: encode and decode change together.
- an O-band burst is named an uplink frame without being decoded
  > The band a burst arrived in is what names its system, and the band is a fact the dwell knew when it armed. Guessing it downstream is what sent every O-band uplink frame into the M-band framer, where it failed and was counted as radio noise.
- an O-band burst of the wrong length is not an uplink frame
- one flipped chip in the sync tail still names the system
  > A dead chip pair in the sync tail must not cost the frame: it is 24 bits long and the two systems differ in far more than one of them.
- the buffer a radio is handed is the burst, less the window the radio inserts
  > The burst two devices on a bench could not exchange: both had written the window into the buffer.
- the constexpr chip coding is the one fec::manchester_encode uses

  > The dwell reads past the end of a short burst, and those chips are nobody's.
- the two sync words share the window the detector matches
  > The trick: two sync words whose chips agree for 18, and a detector armed with 16 of them.
- the window a neighbour missed reads apart from the one noise walked through
  > The 16-chip window is thin enough that the band's own noise walks through it.

### test/core/test_alptas.cpp

ALP-TAS (FLARM-wire, 2024 protocol) air-frame codec. Two things are worth testing here and both are safety-critical: that a frame we transmit is the frame the protocol says (encode -> decode round trip, at several latitudes and across the 20-bit position wrap), and that a frame that is NOT ours is thrown away (frame CRC, plus the decrypt-plausibility gate that is the only thing standing between a mis-framed foreign packet and the traffic table).

**alptas**

- a frame our second refuses names the second its sender keyed
  > The whole of skyblip#2026-09-19: an hour of DEC that was two clocks disagreeing.
- a non-position message type is not decoded as traffic
- a position that unwraps past the pole or the antimeridian is not a target
- a second further out than a clock can plausibly be is not searched for
- aircraft type maps back to the ADS-L category it came from
- altitude round trips including below sea level and high up
- an ICAO address keeps its table, an unknown one degrades to 0
- damage nothing flagged, or too much of it, leaves the frame as it arrived
  > A correction that half-applied would hand the decrypt a frame no transmitter sent.
- encode then decode preserves the identity and motion fields
- encoding refuses to claim a position it does not have
- on-ground and airborne flight state survive the 2-bit field
- position round trips across the 20-bit coding wrap
- position round trips north, south, high latitude and near zero
- speed, climb and track round trip over their ranges
- the airborne field is 1 on the ground, 2 or 3 airborne, and 0 unknown
  > SoftRF latest_encode never sends 0, and reading it as parked silenced the alarm.
- the chip pairs the air destroyed are flipped back to the frame that was sent
- the decrypt gate accepts the second it was sent in, +/-1

### test/core/test_diagnostics.cpp

The status dump, formatted: one line per subsystem for a laptop on the USB console, and the same table as JSON frames for the companion page. Both surfaces come out of comms::DiagnosticsReport, so this is where it is proved that they carry the same numbers under the same names, that neither is ever truncated, and that reading one does not disturb anything.  No device and no link here: a snapshot struct in, characters out.

**diagnostics**

- a counter is never printed negative, on any platform
  > A dump is a witness. A witness that consumes its evidence makes the second reader wrong, which is why every read in the collector is a const accessor and why two reports of the same snapshot are the same bytes. json::Writer carries a long, which is 64-bit on this host and 32-bit on the nRF52. A naked cast would therefore print a negative count on the device and a correct one right here, in the suite we look at. So it saturates, at a ceiling no device reaches: 2147483647 receptions is thirteen years of them at 5 Hz.
- a frame never mixes two subsystems
- a link too narrow for one field is refused, not answered short
  > All or nothing, asked before the first frame goes out: a bench holding four frames of a five-frame dump, with no fifth one coming, has been told that a fault it cannot see is absent.
- a text value is quoted on both surfaces, spaces and all
  > A support case is pasted into an email, so the console form has to stay machine-readable: "CPU LOCKUP" is one value, not two fields.
- no reading is no key, on the console as on the link
- one line per subsystem, each carrying the counters that already exist
- one subsystem can be asked for on its own, under its own name
- reading the dump neither clears nor changes anything
- tenths of a degree round away from zero on both sides of freezing
