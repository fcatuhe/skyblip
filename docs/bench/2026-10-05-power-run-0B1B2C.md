The first power run on silicon, still running when this was written: the charge went unrecorded (#114), and the discharge has 17 hours logged from 96 % to 76 % of the gauge, at a steady parked draw. The run has not reached cutoff, so the pack's capacity is still assumed and not measured.

Before it, the previous discharge ended in a full capture (session 1791055785, not in the repo) that closed on `level` `flat` at 3195 mV on 4 Oct 01:35:48. The cable plugged in after that did not wake the unit, and the reset button did (#113).

## The charge: nothing recorded

From 06:24:02 to 13:21:33, all 836 `power` records read `valid` false, `cell_mv` 0, `external_power` false. The die went from 28.0 to 40.0 C, so the charger was working. A failed read also hides the cable, which is #114. About 7 hours on the cable, from a cell the cutoff had left at 3195 mV under load to 4139 mV just after unplugging. Whether the charger had terminated is not known.

Neither number is a measurement of current. The measured one is only as good as the textbook curve between 4.14 and 3.93 V, which is the flattest part of a LiPo and the part the curve is least sure of. Two readings of it: the table overstates the GNSS receiver or the nRF52840, or the top of the curve gives too few points per volt. Only a run to cutoff tells them apart, because it measures the pack instead of reading the curve. E68BD9's run to cutoff (`2026-10-05-power-run-E68BD9.md`) puts the textbook within 7 points over this voltage range, which is not enough to close the gap. At 40 mA the pack lasts about 60 hours, at 28 mA about 85, against the 35 to 50 hours `core/diag/README.md` planned for.

The `Duty` intervals cover 24 hours, the charge included, because `external_power` never reached the records and `power_budget.py` drops only the intervals that carry it (#114). The modelled average is counters times the table and does not depend on the cable, so it stands.
