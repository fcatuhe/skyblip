# Power run: skyBlip Go 0B1B2C, charge then discharge to cutoff, build 942

The T-Echo Plus ran from 4139 mV just off the cable to the cutoff at 3197 mV in 48 h 57 min, parked. On its 2400 mAh rating that is 47 mA on average, against 40 mA modelled, close to the plain T-Echo's 45 mA (`2026-10-05-power-run-E68BD9.md`). Its discharge curve differs from the T-Echo's, so one table cannot serve both boards. The charge went unrecorded (#114), and the battery level flipped 50 times at its two thresholds (#115).

The previous discharge closed on `level` `flat` at 3195 mV on 4 Oct 01:35:48. Neither the charger nor the button woke the unit after it, only the reset pin did (#113).

## The charge: nothing recorded

From 06:24:02 to 13:21:33, the 836 `power` records on the cable all read `valid` false, `cell_mv` 0 and `external_power` false. The divider sits on the switched supply rail, which the USB lifts to about 4.7 V. 0B1B2C read above the 4700 mV the platform accepts, so every reading was refused, and the cable went unseen with it (#114). The charge took about 7 hours, from the cell the cutoff left at 3195 mV under load to 4139 mV just after unplugging. The die went from 28.0 to 40.0 C.

## The discharge

`scripts/power_budget.py --pack-mah 2400 --session 1791095013`:

| | mA | |
|---|---|---|
| modelled | 40.3 | GNSS 29.0, receive 4.7, nRF52840 3.5, TCXO 2.0, IMU 0.6, the rest under 1 |
| measured | 47.1 | 2304 mAh, 96 points of the gauge, over 48 h 57 min |
| residual | -6.8 | the table explains 86 % of what the cell lost |

The run is not quite whole: its first reading off the cable is 96 %, under the 99 % the script requires. The measured figure assumes the 2400 mAh rating. The `Duty` span is 55 h 55 min because the charge is in it: `external_power` never reached the records, so `power_budget.py` could not drop it. The modelled average does not depend on that.

Against E68BD9, the two runs agree: 48.95 h against 18.49 h is 2.65 times as long, for packs rated 2.82 times apart. So either both units draw about 45 to 47 mA and the table misses 5 to 7 mA, or both packs hold less than their rating. A logging USB meter on the next charge separates the two.

The Plus has no plateau between 3800 and 3750 mV where the T-Echo spent a quarter of its run, and below 3750 mV it holds twice the T-Echo's share at the same voltage. So `kLowMv` (3600) came 10.3 h before the cutoff on the Plus and 1.8 h on the T-Echo, and `kCriticalMv` (3500) 5.0 h against 47 min. Whether that is the pack or the load is open: the Plus draws about C/51 from its pack, the T-Echo about C/19. Either way the gauge needs a curve per board, and the board already tells them apart at bring-up.

### The levels flicker at each threshold

The level flipped 21 times between `normal` and `low` from 6 Oct 03:28 to 04:25 UTC, at 3587 to 3610 mV, and 29 times between `low` and `critical` from 08:52 to 09:42, at 3478 to 3515 mV. That is far worse than the T-Echo's 8 flips, because the Plus crosses each band more slowly. Each flip moves the lamp, the radar ring and the status row. #115 is the fix: climbing a step now needs the reading 35 mV above it.
