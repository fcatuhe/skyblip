# Power run: skyBlip Go 0B1B2C, second charge then discharge to cutoff, build 987

The same T-Echo Plus, parked, ran from 4134 mV just off the cable to the cutoff at 3193 mV in 49 h 59 min, 2 % longer than its first run (`2026-10-05-power-run-0B1B2C.md`), against the same 40.3 mA modelled. Its discharge curve repeats the first run's within one point at every voltage, as the plain T-Echo's did (`2026-10-14-power-run-E68BD9.md`), so on both boards one run gives the unit's curve. Build 987 fixes the two faults the first run found: the cable is seen on every record, and each battery level is entered once against 50 flips. The charge left no cell reading, but the die temperature and the rail mark where it ended, about 7 hours after the plug. The boot after the cutoff kept `went_dark_flat`, where E68BD9's lost it (#125).

The unit's previous capture, a `FULL` one, closed on 11 Oct 20:47:22 at 3624 mV and 13 %. Nothing was recorded between that and the boot on the cable, so the charge started from a cell of unknown level: flat if the unit stayed on, since 13 % lasts about 7 hours at this draw.

## The charge

From 07:14:03 to 17:37:33, 10 h 23 min, the 1248 `power` records on the cable all read `external_power` and `charging` true, `valid` false, `percent` 0 and `level` `normal`, with the divider on the USB rail at 4686 to 4837 mV. #114 is fixed on this unit too. The first reading off the cable, 30 s after the last on it, is 4134 mV, `valid`, 97 %.

This rail sits above the 4700 mV ceiling the whole time, so every reading was refused, 60 a minute, and the refusals cannot mark the end of the charge as they did on E68BD9. The die and the rail median do:

The die went from 22.7 C at the plug to 39.0 C and held 38 to 39 C until about 10:35. The rail fell 35 mV over the same stretch, then rose 100 mV as the charge current tapered, and both settled about 14:15, 7 hours after the plug. On E68BD9 the same marks put the end 2.3 to 2.6 h after the plug. If both started flat, that ratio of 2.5 to 3 is the ratio of the two ratings, 2.82, at the same charge current. Neither start is known, and no current was measured.

## The discharge

`scripts/power_budget.py --pack-mah 2400 --session 1791875613`:

| | mA | |
|---|---|---|
| modelled | 40.3 | GNSS 29.0, receive 4.7, nRF52840 3.5, TCXO 2.0, IMU 0.6, the rest under 1 |
| measured | 46.6 | 2328 mAh, 97 points of the gauge, over 49 h 59 min |
| residual | -6.3 | the table explains 86 % of what the cell lost |

The run is not quite whole: its first reading off the cable is 97 %, under the 99 % the script requires. On the 2400 mAh rating alone, from just off the cable to the cutoff, the two runs draw 48.0 mA (this one) and 49.0 mA (the first). The model integrates to 2014 mAh here, 84 % of the rating, and to 749 mAh on E68BD9's second run, 88 % of its 850. So both boards show the same gap: either the table misses 5 to 7 mA on both, or both packs hold about 85 % of their rating.

The two runs differ in small ways, none of which explains 2 %: no Bluetooth link here against 101 s, 8 partial refreshes an hour against 40, and a die 1.5 C cooler (median 26.7 C against 28.2 C). Air time is identical, 30 ms of transmit a minute, half E68BD9's, so this unit sends no callsign.

At fixed shares left, as #119 proposes, the ladder would move on this board: 15 % left is about 3550 mV and 5 % about 3430 mV, just above the 3400 mV boot lockout. On E68BD9 the same shares are about 3675 and 3520 mV.

## The boot after the cutoff kept `went_dark_flat`

On the cable after the cutoff, the unit came up with `reset` `BUTTON WAKE`, and the status reply said `went_dark_flat` true. E68BD9, woken by the cable alone and reporting `RESET PIN`, lost the flag (#125), so the flag survives a button wake and the fault is in the wake the cable causes.
