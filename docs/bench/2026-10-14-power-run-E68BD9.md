# Power run: skyBlip Go E68BD9, second charge then discharge to cutoff, build 987

The same plain T-Echo, parked, ran from 4160 mV just off the cable to the cutoff at 3192 mV in 18 h 51 min, 2 % longer than its first run (`2026-10-05-power-run-E68BD9.md`), against the same 39.7 mA modelled. Its discharge curve repeats the first run's within two points at every voltage, so on this unit the curve is the cell's and not the run's. Build 987 fixes what the first run found: the cable is seen on every record and each battery level is entered once. The charge left no cell reading, but the die temperature and the refused readings both mark where it ended. The boot that followed the cutoff forgot `went_dark_flat` (#125).

The unit's previous capture, a `FULL` one on build 983, closed on 11 Oct 20:56:46 at 3879 mV and 68 %. Nothing was recorded between that and the boot on the cable, so the charge started from a cell of unknown level: flat if the unit stayed on, since 68 % lasts about 13 hours at this draw.

## The charge

From 07:14:49 to 17:37:49, 10 h 23 min, the 1247 `power` records on the cable all read `external_power` and `charging` true, `valid` false, `percent` 0 and `level` `normal`, with the divider on the USB rail at 4665 to 4739 mV. #114 is fixed on this unit: the cable is seen on every pass, and no rail reading reaches the gauge. The first reading off the cable, 30 s after the last on it, is 4160 mV, `valid`, 97 %.

The board has no charger status pin, so nothing says when the charge ended. Two records mark it anyway, every 20 minutes:

| 07:14 | 31.8 C | 4694 mV | 8.5 a minute |
| 07:54 | 37.0 C | 4693 mV | 8.7 a minute |
| 08:34 | 35.0 C | 4693 mV | 9.7 a minute |
| 08:54 | 30.9 C | 4700 mV | 22.5 a minute |
| 09:14 | 28.2 C | 4702 mV | 33.3 a minute |
| 09:34 | 26.3 C | 4708 mV | 45.6 a minute |
| 10:14 to 17:14 | 26.0 to 27.1 C | 4705 to 4712 mV | 44 to 49 a minute |

The die went from 21.5 C at the plug to 37.2 C, held there for about an hour, and was back at the room's 26 C by about 09:35. The rail rose about 15 mV as the charge current fell, which pushed most of its readings over the 4700 mV ceiling and multiplied the `implausible` count by five. Both put the end of the charge 2.3 to 2.6 h after the plug. They are proxies: no current was measured, and the starting level is unknown. The `implausible` count reached 24490 on the cable, so it says nothing about the divider on a unit that has been charged.

## The discharge

`scripts/power_budget.py --pack-mah 850 --session 1791875652`:

| | mA | |
|---|---|---|
| modelled | 39.7 | GNSS 29.0, receive 4.7, nRF52840 3.5, TCXO 2.0, the rest under 1 |
| measured | 43.7 | 824 mAh, 97 points of the gauge, over 18 h 51 min |
| residual | -4.0 | the table explains 91 % of what the cell lost |

The run is not quite whole: its first reading off the cable is 97 %, under the 99 % the script requires. On the 850 mAh rating alone, from just off the cable to the cutoff, the two runs draw 45.1 mA (this one) and 46.0 mA (the first). The model integrates to 749 mAh here and 735 mAh on the first run. So the two runs agree within 2 %, and the open question stays open: either the table misses about 5 mA, or this pack holds about 750 mAh.

The two runs differ in small ways, none of which explains 2 %: no Bluetooth link here against 137 s, 3.5 partial refreshes an hour against 11.6, and a die 2.7 C cooler (median 25.5 C against 28.2 C). Air time is identical, 60 ms of transmit a minute and the receiver armed 98.8 % of the time.

Each level was entered once and never left, against 8 flips on the first run: #115's 35 mV margin works. The margin's premise holds too: the reading spreads 33.5 mV inside a half hour at the median of this run's half-hour windows, against 35 mV on the first run.

## The boot after the cutoff forgot `went_dark_flat`

The owner plugged the cable into the unit the cutoff had switched off and did nothing else. It booted: 135 s later the diagnostics reply said `reset` `RESET PIN`, `charging` true, and the status reply said `went_dark_flat` false. `core/power/README.md` says otherwise twice: the cable alone wakes nothing, because the factory bootloader sends a VBUS wake back to SYSTEM OFF, and the boot that follows a flat cutoff answers `went_dark_flat` true, once. The boot that opened this run, on the cable on 13 Oct, carries the same `RESET PIN` reason.
